# Player Matchup Drilldown

The Fantasy Matchups individual-player drilldown is an evidence-led weekly decision briefing. It classifies the available outlook as Strong, Favorable, Mixed, Risky, or Very risky and explains that classification with visible numbers. It does not issue an absolute start/sit command or recommend the player over a specific roster alternative.

## Views and ownership

- `src/components/companion/PlayerMatchupBreakdown.jsx` owns the dialog, its phase-derived layout, hero, linked week/stat-line state, sections and disclosures. Its scoped styles live in `PlayerMatchupBreakdown.css`: the dashboard uses `pdd-*` classes and the shared header, strip and disclosures keep `pmd-*`. Only elements from the dashboard design ship; the earlier slot system, headline sentence, conditions tile, form ladder, projected stat-line table, scoring breakdown table and season/defense splits panel were removed.
- `src/components/companion/drilldown/DrilldownCharts.jsx` owns the SVG charts (likely outcome, projection build, defense tiers, linked week columns, scoring pace/game flow scrub, rings, stat-line bars, defense rank strip); `drilldownChartUtils.js` holds their formatting helpers and the container-width hook. Charts are drawn at their container's real pixel width and fall back to 360px for the server render.
- `src/utils/playerDrilldownModel.js` shapes the dashboard data: week rows, stat-key selection and reconciled stat lines, the projection bridge, scoring pace, elapsed fraction and the next-week preview. It introduces no new projection or scoring source.

Before the player's own NFL kickoff, only the projection-led briefing is shown: the projection against the season average, the season rank and availability status, projected stat line, likely outcome, recent form, defense tier and projection build. Venue, home/away and available weather stay in the phase strip. The expected range reuses the shared projection's historical 25th-to-75th-percentile profile and is never described as a guaranteed floor or ceiling.

There is no performance view switcher. Game phase alone selects the content, so the dialog always reflects the player's actual state without the user choosing a mode. Live shows accumulating fantasy points, the delta against the elapsed-game expected pace target, and estimated play contributions; the full-game projection remains the disclosed benchmark tick. Final shows the settled total, its delta against the full-game projection, and the biggest scoring contributions as evidence cells. The retained pregame projection is used when one exists; otherwise a shared projection appears as an explicitly labelled **Available estimate** and is not treated as a pregame comparison. Missing reported points remain unavailable; a reported zero is displayed as zero. A total that has not been reported states that explicitly rather than rendering as a dash alone.

The hero number carries its own tone: during a live game it is green when the actual score is at or above the points expected by that point in the game, and red when it is behind that pace. At final it is green above the full-game projection and red below it. This is deliberate visual cueing against the appropriate disclosed benchmark, and it replaces the earlier neutral-final-score rule.

The header and phase strip remain outside the scrolling body, and the body is the single scrolling region so expanded disclosures stay reachable. At final the strip's second row shows the settled NFL score with the winning side emphasised, taken from the schedule entry's `ptsFor`/`ptsAgainst` (populated only for completed games), and the first row drops the kickoff time for the date alone. The header uses the canonical NFL team visual theme, keeps injury/availability beside player identity, and retains the existing statistics-navigation actions. The context row shows the known local kickoff, opponent identity, venue, home/away state, and available outdoor weather. When the caller passes `onViewScheduleGame` (Matchups does, via App's `navigateToStatisticsScheduleGame`), the strip's matchup row is a button in every phase: it closes the drilldown and opens that NFL game's drill-in on Statistics › Schedule in By Week mode, keyed `season-Wweek-AWAY-HOME`. Week mode is used because the schedule compares that id with team spellings normalized (WAS/WSH, LA/LAR), so the fantasy platform's abbreviations still find the ESPN row; By Team would need the schedule feed's own team id. Before kickoff, when the player carries an injury designation and the caller passes `onOpenInjuries` (Matchups does on Sleeper leagues, via App's `openFantasyInjuriesPlayer`), the hero's Status tile is a button that closes the drilldown and opens that player on Fantasy › Injuries. An untagged player's tile reads Active and links nowhere.

The body follows the Claude Design "Player Drilldown Dashboard" study. A hero row pairs the key number with the phase chart: the **Projected stat line** before kickoff (followed in the evidence sections by Likely outcome, Recent form, Vs defense tier and Projection build, in that order; in the one-column layout under 720px, Recent form is lifted with CSS `order` to sit directly under the stat line, while two columns already place it under the stat line in the right column), **Scoring pace** while live, **Game flow** once settled. The hero number is a button that opens a points popover (where the projected, live or final points come from by stat). Below a rule, live and final show stat rings (actual against the projected stat line), then evidence sections sit in one column on phones and two columns with a hairline divider once the dialog's container reaches 720px. Disclosures close the body. The layout is driven by container queries on the dialog, not viewport media queries. On phones the mobile sheet runs to 24px below the safe area (`--modal-mobile-sheet-max-height` on `.matchup-breakdown-dialog`), and a dialog container of 560px or less tightens the header, strip and section spacing, sets the key tiles beside the hero number (separated by a hairline) instead of under it, lays the stat rings out in one row with their labels below, and runs stat lines (Projected stat line, Points vs projection, Stat line) two stats to a row: each stat is a 44px filter tile with label and points on one line and the bar underneath, the Total row keeps the full width, and an 8px row gap keeps neighbouring tap areas from overlapping. In the same dialog the charts draw a compact geometry (`useChartWidth` returns `compact` when the dialog is 560px or narrower, keyed off the dialog so desktop's packed columns keep full spacing): Likely outcome 100→76px, Vs defense tier 168→128px, Recent form/Season trend 184→148px, scoring pace/game flow 210→176px, and tighter row pitch and top padding in Projection build; legends, notes and the defense rank strip tighten with them. Nothing is hidden at phone width, and coarse-pointer targets keep 44px.

**Empty evidence is left out at every width.** Before kickoff, Likely outcome needs at least one played game or a likely range (otherwise it would only repeat the hero's projection-vs-average line), Recent form needs at least one played game (the projected column alone repeats the hero number), and the Vs defense tier chart needs a game in at least one tier (the opponent rank strip still shows on its own). The week-columns chart reserves its opponent row only when a column has an opponent. The phase strip omits the logo slot when there is no opponent, and a date-only `gameDate` fallback is shown as a local date only when it falls in the league season (Matchups can pass a placeholder from a fixed 2025 week-date table when the league's schedule entry is missing). Covered by the `sparse=1` harness mode in `tests/drilldown/player-drilldown.spec.js`.

Hero tiles: pregame shows the season rank and availability status; live shows the pace-adjusted **Est. finish** (`getPaceAdjustedProjection`); final shows the week finish and the settled season average with its change from the pre-game average. The hero number opens its points breakdown on hover with a mouse, on tap with touch, and on keyboard focus. **Likely outcome** places the last five games, the likely range band, the season average and the projection on one scale; each past game opens a tooltip with its stat summary, defense tier and result, and selecting it filters by that game (selecting it again returns to this week).

**Past-week filter (pregame).** Selecting a played week in Recent form or Likely outcome is one shared selection. The hero switches to that week's fantasy points against the season average (its popover to that week's stat line), the rank tile to that week's positional finish (`computeWeeklyPositionalRanks`), Likely outcome marks the game, Recent form selects the column, and Vs defense tier highlights that game's tier and opponent instead of this week's. Only a Recent form pick also swaps Projection build for **Week N build** (season-average points → that week's points by category, `buildWeekCategoryShift`); a Likely outcome pick leaves the projection build alone. Vs defense tier is not itself a filter, and its rank strip always describes this week's opponent. Selecting the projected column returns everything to this week.

The rank slot is the player's rank by total season fantasy points before and during the game. Once the NFL week has fully concluded it switches to the already-computed weekly positional rank (`weekRank`) and is labelled **Week N finish**. Projected finish, starter-pool rank, and rank movement since kickoff are still not inferred.

**Recent form** (pregame: last five games plus this week's projection as a dashed ghost column) and **Season trend** (final: last six games with this week outlined) are linked to the stat line. Selecting a week shows its stat line; selecting a stat row re-scales the week columns to that stat's points; columns are green at or above the season average and red below, with the average drawn as a moving line. Deductions (lost fumbles, interceptions, sacks) plot below a zero line that moves with the scale, with their value under the bar; covered by the deduction-filter check in `tests/drilldown/player-drilldown.spec.js` (week 3 of the harness fixture carries a lost fumble). The stat line uses the five stat keys carrying the most fantasy points across the shown weeks and the projection; anything else is kept as **Other scoring** so rows always add to the week total. At final the stat line reads **Points vs projection** for this week (ticks at the projected line) and **Points vs season avg** for earlier weeks. **Usage** (final) shows offensive snap share (`off_snp / tm_off_snp`) for the selected week against the season average and can filter the trend by snap share; it appears only when snap counts exist. GridShift does not retain historical weekly projections, so earlier weeks compare with the season average.

**Projection build** (pregame) explains how the projection is reached from the season average. A BALLDONTLIE projection (then Sleeper) is a projected stat line with no published reasoning, so the section shows each scoring category as a row running from the player's season-average points to the projected points (`CategoryShiftChart`), on one scale with zero marked, plus a numbers-only Total row. Showing both ends instead of a signed delta keeps deductions readable: Turnovers (the `negative` breakdown group) stay below zero on both sides, and a smaller deduction shows as a green move toward zero. The row's line and projected value are green when the category is projected higher, red when lower. The provider name is not shown. GridShift's own model is multiplicative, so its adjustments are attributed in a fixed order — recent form, small-sample shrinkage, matchup, usage trend (when enabled), weather, availability — and each bar is the change at that step; home/away is omitted because the model's location factor is fixed at 1. The model build names its source (GridShift model).

**Vs defense tier** (pregame) draws the receiving- (rushing- for RBs) defense tiers with the opponent's tier highlighted, and a 32-team strip of the opponent's fantasy-points-allowed rank with the allowance and league average.

**Next week** (final) shows GridShift's early model estimate for the following game (no provider projection exists for a future week yet) and that opponent's fantasy-points-allowed rank, or the bye.

Disclosures are full-width rows with a +/− marker, as in the design. Pregame: **How projections work** (projection source, likely-range definition, how the projection build reads) and **Opponent sample** (evidence period, allowance against the league average, tier definition). Live: the **Pregame projection** or **Available estimate** with its value, and **About play points**. Final: **All scoring plays** and the projection.

Blocks the design study specifies but GridShift cannot source — live rank movement, teammate target share, remaining opportunity, route and target share by week, per-week projection history — render as explicitly labelled dashed placeholders only when `VITE_DRILLDOWN_SLOTS=true` in a dev build (`src/utils/drilldownDevSlots.js`). They never ship to users and are never faked with invented values.

## Weekly outlook and opponent context

`buildPlayerOutlook()` and the headline sentence are no longer rendered by the drilldown; the utilities remain in `playerMatchupPresentation.js` for their tests and any future surface.

Opponent fantasy points allowed uses active league scoring and ranks lower allowance as tougher. Equal allowance values share the same competition rank. The direct comparison is opponent allowance versus the league-average allowance from that same evidence set. Through the first four current-season games, `buildDrilldownOpponentContext()` progressively replaces a prior-season baseline with current-season results at 25 percentage points per observed game. The surface explicitly labels Current season, Prior-season context, or Early-season blend and discloses both samples in the methodology expansion. Prior data must have at least three games; unsupported one- or two-game current samples do not stand alone.

This blend is isolated to the drilldown's editorial context. It does not change the shared projection's opponent factor, `defStrength` / `defPercentile`, Fantasy Heatmap aggregation, or `playerDefensePerformance` yardage classifications. Previous-season stats are loaded through Week 4 only to support this labeled briefing context and the existing projection fallback.

## Recorded pregame projections

`useMatchupProjectionBaselines.js` captures the latest projection observed while Fantasy Matchups is open and the player's known kickoff is still in the future. It captures displayed starters and bench players, without requiring the individual drilldown to open.

`matchupProjectionBaseline.js` scopes each record to league, season, week, player, and a canonical fingerprint of the active scoring settings. It stores projection/stat-line/source/range details plus capture time and scheduled kickoff. Started/completed games, unknown kickoff, and unavailable/non-finite scores cannot create baselines. No live or historical projection is presented as a reconstructed pregame baseline.

Records are local to the browser/device. Each observation has a separate localStorage key under `gridshift-matchup-projection-baselines-v2:`. Reading chooses the newest valid observation per scope; pruning retains the latest 400 scopes. Separate observation keys prevent unrelated concurrent tabs from replacing one aggregate store, and a delayed older observation cannot replace a newer timestamp. Scoring changes require a matching pregame record. Blocked storage falls back to memory for the session. This is not a server archive or cross-device sync; unobserved/evicted historical baselines remain unavailable.

## Heatmap consistency and defense measures

`src/utils/fantasyHeatmapData.js` extracts the existing Heatmap offensive aggregation unchanged. Both Heatmap and the new analysis consume it. Its table is keyed by the offense producing the stats, uses Heatmap's existing positive-value semantics, and uses the same historical team attribution/fallback. The drilldown follows the reciprocal NFL schedule to derive what each opposing defense allowed.

The measures are **receiving yards allowed** and **rushing yards allowed** per completed game across all supported NFL offensive positions. These are explicit Heatmap yardage measures, not a new composite pass-defense rating. Compare with Heatmap using the same weeks, all offensive positions, and the opposing offensive team for each game. Missing team/week yardage data is unavailable, never a zero. NFL defense quartiles remain unavailable until the selected period has observed coverage for the full defense pool. Explicitly reported zero yardage remains zero.

`src/utils/playerDefensePerformance.js` takes only current-season stats and fully completed NFL weeks, as verified by the shared `isFullGameWeekComplete`. In-progress or incomplete slates are excluded. The same current classification through the latest completed week is applied to all past opponents; it is not the defense's historical rank before each game.

Lower allowed yardage ranks as stronger. The strongest quarter, middle half, and weakest quarter use tied-value midpoint placement so tied defenses are not split arbitrarily. A current opponent's measurement includes its rank, defense count, yards allowed per game, and sample size. Offensive defense splits apply to QB/RB/WR/TE; defensive players and kickers retain positional performance context without these offensive splits.

## Player rankings and evidence

The primary season-points rank comes from Fantasy Matchups' existing positional ranking and is always labelled **Season points rank** and **by total season fantasy points**. Its pool includes only players with recorded fantasy-stat fields, but it retains valid zero and negative fantasy totals; players with no recorded fantasy stats are excluded rather than treated as zero. It is distinct from the deeper current-season fantasy-points-per-game analysis below.

Player performance is fantasy points per qualifying game under active league scoring, using `calcPoints` with the player's position. It retains zero and negative fantasy games and requires a valid scheduled opponent. Explicit zero games played and bye/phantom rows are excluded. Ties share rank. These PPG rankings have a different, explicitly named metric from existing total-points positional finishes.

Peers are NFL players at the same position regardless of fantasy ownership. QB peer eligibility requires explicit starter metadata (depth-chart QB order 1 or a recorded start in the completed period). The selected player never joins that pool solely because they were selected. When starter eligibility or league-wide starter coverage is unavailable, factual target results remain visible but the rank stays unavailable.

Results and game counts use whatever current-season data is available once at least one game in the selected week has finished; earlier weeks continue to require complete-week evidence. Alongside fantasy PPG, common position-specific stats such as passing yards, rushing yards, receptions, touchdowns, field goals, or extra points receive the same factual ranking treatment. Rank markers require at least three qualifying games for the fantasy-PPG strip; stat rankings show the available sample and pool directly. A compact ranking strip shows relative value, rank, pool count, and sample size. Each defense category can expand to show the contributing weeks/opponents/points. The current season period is visible.

## Estimated play contributions

While the player's game is live, `usePlayerMatchupTimeline.js` loads the selected player's supported play-by-play feed and `playerMatchupTimeline.js` converts attributable plays into estimated signed fantasy-point contributions under the active league scoring. Positive and negative events are retained in chronological order; zero-contribution events are omitted. The official headline fantasy total and `buildFantasyScoringBreakdown` remain authoritative.

The live **Scoring plays** section is labelled **What earned the points** and always discloses that bonuses, corrections, and missing plays can differ from the official total. The same events place each play on the game clock in **Scoring pace** (live, with the expected pace line, likely-range cone and pace-adjusted finish) and **Game flow** (final, fetched once without polling, with every play listed under **All scoring plays**). The official total stays authoritative: any gap between the play sum and the official total is drawn as a labelled stat update at "now". Loading, empty, unavailable, error, and stale states remain explicit.

**Without BALLDONTLIE.** The play feed is the drilldown's only BALLDONTLIE-only input. On a server with no BDL key, `/api/live/status` reports `apiKeyReady: false` and the loader returns unavailable with "Play-by-play isn’t set up on this server, so scoring plays can’t be shown." before any session, game or play request; an unreachable live server shows the API client's error instead. Projections fall back to Sleeper, then the GridShift model, then none. Without loaded plays the pace chart still draws from the official total and projection, but the play-only cues (the TD legend and the "stat updates not matched to a play" note) are hidden, since every point would otherwise read as unmatched. With no projection, How projections work says so instead of describing the model.

## Higher projected bench option

Before kickoff, `playerMatchupBenchOption.js` may return one informational bench comparison when the selected starter belongs to the user and the highest eligible bench player's projection is at least 2.0 points higher. Both players need known future kickoffs. Bye, unavailable, inactive, injured-reserve, taxi, and position-ineligible candidates are excluded. Eligibility is a direct fit for the selected starter slot; the model does not chain multiple lineup moves.

The callout is labelled **Higher projected option on your bench**, shows the candidate and projected difference, and opens that player's drilldown. It never changes the fantasy-platform lineup. The callout disappears when either relevant game reaches kickoff.
